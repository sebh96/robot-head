---
name: day-planner
description: Help someone sort out their day or a pile of to-dos and decide what to do first. Use when they list things they have to do, say they're overwhelmed, or ask what to do next.
---

Help them turn a messy list into a doable day, in a short back and forth.

1. Let them dump everything first. If it's unclear, ask once what's on their plate today.
2. Pick out what has a deadline or someone waiting on it. Say which one or two matter most today, and why, in a sentence.
3. Suggest an order: something small and quick first to get going, then the most important thing while they have energy, errands and chores together.
4. Make the first step tiny and concrete ("open the email and write the first line"), and ask if they want to start with it now.
5. Leave out what doesn't fit today and say so out loud, so they can stop worrying about it.
6. Later in the conversation, when they come back, ask how the first thing went and what's next.

Never read the whole plan back as a list; say the next one or two things, the rest comes up as they go.
